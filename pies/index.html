• This chart gives users a type of overview of the productions offered in the archive while allowing them to dive deeper into the ways region, country, and language interact with each other within productions.

    Region is the left-most pie and broadest category. Region largely refers to the cultural traditions behind a particular production. The archive has several regional editors who provide more context on these cultural traditions and are used to define the regions used in the chart.

    Country is the center and largest pie. Country refers to the location in which a production was performed in the case of a stage play or produced in the case of a film adaptation. Though the location of a performance and the cultural tradition behind it don't always correlate (think a Hindi-language play set in Mumbai but performed in London), country is oftentimes a used way to drill deeper into a production's background.

    Language is the right-most and smallest pie. Language simply refers to the language used in a production. Currently, one production that utilizes multiple languages is double-counted. Again, language and performance country don't necessarily relate in the ways you would expect, but this organization still provides some intersting insights.

    Clicking a slice of a broader pie will reveal the narrower pie of the correlating pie. You can also choose to show and hide the pie slice labels to make viewing easier.
